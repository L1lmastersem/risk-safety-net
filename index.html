Trading Research

Most trading strategies online come with impressive backtests. I rebuilt them in TradingView (Pine Script) and Python, tested every one against the same fixed rules, and built a tool to check what happens when several strategies share one account.

86 strategies, one testing standard. Below is a selection of the results, including the failures, because those taught me the most.

86 strategies tested in detail
3 passed every rule
3 markets: crypto, index futures and gold

The testing standard

  1. No lookahead. Higher-timeframe data is only read from candles that have already closed. A backtest that knows the future always looks great.
  2. Real costs. Exchange fees, overnight fees and slippage are always on. Results without fees don't count.
  3. Rules fixed before the run. Pass and fail criteria are written down first, and settings don't change after seeing the result.
  4. Enough trades, in both halves. A result needs a real sample, and the first and second half of the data both have to hold up.
  5. A second market. Where possible, the same settings have to work on a symbol the strategy wasn't picked on.

Selected results

PF = profit factor: gross profit ÷ gross loss. Above 1 makes money, below 1 loses it. The bar to pass was 1.3 after costs.

What I learned

A strategy can look safe on each chart separately and still break an account's daily loss limit when several symbols lose on the same day. Baseline equity With safety net

How the safety net works: Layer 1 shrinks each new trade as the day's combined loss grows, so its worst case can't push the day past the limit. Layer 2 caps any single trade at a fixed share of the daily limit, which catches one oversized first trade of the day.

How risk per trade is estimated: a trade export has each trade's outcome but not its original stop distance. The tool uses a losing trade's loss directly and half a winning trade's gain as a rough stand-in. Treat results as directionally correct, not exact to the dollar.

Sample data: two made-up symbols over one year of trading days, generated in the browser. It is not real trading data.
